The experiment successfully answered a feasibility-plus-physiology question, not a synaptic-glutamate question. Isolated mouse retina, on a flexible parylene-C shank, can yield a slowly sampled Fc–aptamer ACV signal that moves with room-light on/off in the direction expected for basal extracellular glutamate, on the same insertion as ganglion-cell spikes. Stronger than a typical bath-apply tissue demo. Weaker than a chemically identified quantitative tissue assay: 2/4 probes fail the light test, Ames quantification is poor, no pharmacology, authors decline millisecond synaptic readout.

Successful question: Can a thiol/Fc glutamate aptamer on an Au-nanostructured parylene-C intraretinal probe report light-dependent, steady-state changes in a glutamate-correlated electrochemical signal inside isolated mouse retina, while a neighboring electrode on the same shank records ganglion-cell spikes?

Author answer, qualified: yes, for basal Glu averaged over ~1 min, not synaptic transients.

Key quotes:
- “the aptasensor captures sustained changes in basal Glu concentration, rather than the fast, transient release events associated with individual synaptic activity.”
- “the time resolution of the electrochemical aptasensor limits the detection of rapid, transient Glu release events, which typically occur on the millisecond to second scale in vitro. Despite this, the aptasensor was able to reliably resolve steady-state differences in extracellular Glu concentration between light and dark conditions.”

Licensed: relative, slow, light-locked signal-gain changes on Probe 1 and 2 (p < 0.05), concurrent with ON-like RGC firing on Probe 1. Not absolute [Glu], not cleft kinetics, not in vivo.
